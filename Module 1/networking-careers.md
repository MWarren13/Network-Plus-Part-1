### Research Questions

1. **What kinds of networking jobs exist today?**  
   Look into roles like network administrator, cloud network engineer, and data center technician. What do these jobs involve?

   A Network Administrator is in charge of the everyday operations of a company’s network. They deal with installing and setting up routers and switches, managing user access, and troubleshooting issues. They also make sure the network stays secure and reliable.

   A Cloud Network Engineer works with virtual environments instead of physical hardware. Their job focuses on building and maintaining networks in the cloud using platforms like AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud. They make sure cloud services can communicate safely and efficiently. They often use automation tools to deploy and manage these systems.

   A Data Center Technician is more hands-on. They physically install and maintain servers, switches, and cabling inside a data center. If equipment goes down, they swap out parts and make sure everything is up and running again.

2. **How has the rise of cloud computing changed networking careers?**  
   Explore how companies like AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud have centralized many networking functions.

   Cloud computing has changed networking careers by shifting much of the work from physical hardware to virtual environments. Instead of configuring switches and routers on-site, professionals are now setting up virtual private clouds, subnets, and secure connections online. This change requires new skills, such as cloud platform knowledge, automation, and scripting, while still keeping the fundamentals of traditional networking important. In many ways, cloud computing has expanded the role of networking professionals rather than replaced it, since they now have to manage both on-premises systems and cloud-based networks.

3. **Why are smaller organizations using hybrid cloud networks?**  
   Investigate how businesses combine on-premises infrastructure with cloud services, and what challenges they face.

   It allows them to keep important data and systems on-premises while still taking advantage of the flexibility of the cloud. This setup helps balance security, cost, and scalability, but it also creates challenges. Many small businesses struggle with the complexity of connecting local infrastructure to cloud services, and limited IT staff can make it harder to manage. Security is another concern since data moves between environments, but overall hybrid networks give smaller organizations a practical middle ground between control and flexibility.

4. **How do laws and regulations affect how networks are built?**  
   Search for examples of compliance requirements (like HIPAA or GDPR) that influence network design.

   Laws and regulations shape how networks are built because companies must design systems that protect sensitive data. For instance, HIPAA requires strong security for patient records, and GDPR forces organizations to safeguard personal information and sometimes keep it in specific regions. These rules add complexity and cost, but they ensure networks are secure, compliant, and trustworthy.

5. **What impact is AI having on networking?**  
   Find out how automation, machine learning, and AI tools are changing how networks are monitored and managed.

   AI is starting to change networking by making it more automated and smarter. Tools powered by AI and machine learning can monitor network traffic, detect problems, and even fix issues before they cause downtime. For example, AI can identify unusual activity that might be a security threat or predict when hardware is about to fail so it can be replaced ahead of time. Automation also reduces the need for IT teams to do repetitive tasks like configuring devices or managing updates, letting them focus on more complex problems. Overall, AI is helping networks become faster, more reliable, and easier to manage.
